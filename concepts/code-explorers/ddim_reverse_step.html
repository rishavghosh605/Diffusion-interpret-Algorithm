What scheduler.step actually computes

self.scheduler.step(...) turns \(\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)\) into \(x_{t-1}\). This is DDIM's version of it, with \(\eta=0\): deterministic, no randomness, the case the X-Ray asks you to read first.

How self.scheduler.step runs

Where it sits in the sampling algorithm

Ho et al. (2020), Algorithm 2, written in the survey's notation. DDIM reaches the same final row as DDPM, by a different route.

DDIM does not compute \(\mu_\theta(x_t,t)\) at all; row 4 is not on this page. Instead it predicts \(\hat x_0\) directly (lines 455 to 457) and recombines it deterministically (lines 488 and 491), reaching row 5 by a different equation, Song et al.'s Eq. 12, not the survey's Eq. 6. With \(\eta=0\), \(\Sigma_\theta\) is not sampled from at all: the step is a fixed function of \(\epsilon_\theta\), nothing else.

What this means for your project

With \(\eta=0\) this function is a fixed, deterministic transform of whatever \(\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)\) you hand it: same noise prediction in, same \(x_{t-1}\) out, every time. It adds no randomness and makes no decisions of its own.

That means it is not a place to intervene. The noise prediction (the UNet forward, a separate page) stays the only point where swapping conditioning or latent representations changes anything; this page is what happens automatically, deterministically, afterward.

Diffusion terms

The ideas the math is built from.

Forward process, survey Eq. 3
The closed form that skips straight from \(x_0\) to any \(x_t\): \(x_t=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon_t\). Inverting this for \(x_0\) is exactly derivation 1 below, and exactly what lines 455 to 457 compute.
Per-step \(\alpha_t,\beta_t\) versus cumulative \(\bar\alpha_t,\bar\beta_t\)
The noise schedule fixes \(\beta_t\) directly, one value per training timestep (SD 1.5: \(\sqrt{\beta_t}\) linear from \(\sqrt{0.00085}\) to \(\sqrt{0.012}\) over 1000 steps); \(\alpha_t:=1-\beta_t\) is just that one step's complement, how much signal a single forward step keeps. \(\bar\alpha_t:=\prod_{i=1}^t\alpha_i\) is the cumulative product of every per-step \(\alpha_i\) up to \(t\), how much signal survives from \(x_0\) all the way to \(x_t\); \(\bar\beta_t:=1-\bar\alpha_t\) is its complement, this page's own shorthand, not the same number as any single \(\beta_t\). Both pairs satisfy the same "1 minus the other" relation at their own scale, which is the easy place to mix them up.
Predicted \(x_0\), \(\hat x_0\)
The network's one-shot guess at the clean image, computed from \(x_t\) and \(\hat\epsilon\) without taking any reverse steps. Code name pred_original_sample. Clipped to \([-1,1]\) by default (clip_sample=True) before being used further.
DDIM's reverse step, Song et al. 2021, Eq. 12
\(x_{t-1}=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t-1}}\,\hat x_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_{t-1}-\sigma_t^2}\,\hat\epsilon+\sigma_t\,z\), \(z\sim\mathcal N(0,I)\). Lines 488 and 491 compute the \(\eta=0\) case, where \(\sigma_t=0\) and the \(\sigma_t z\) term drops out entirely: no \(z\) is ever drawn. Not in the survey, which only states Ho et al.'s DDPM update (Eq. 6); the survey's glossary entry on this page's series notes DDIM and DDPM share the same trained network but differ here.
\(\eta\) (eta)
The knob that interpolates between DDIM (\(\eta=0\), deterministic, this page) and DDPM (\(\eta=1\), fully stochastic): std_dev_t = eta * variance ** 0.5, line 481. \(0\) by default in __call__. Derivation 3 shows why \(\eta=1\) exactly recovers DDPM's own reverse variance.
v_prediction, survey Eq. 8
An alternative training target to raw noise: \(v:=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon-\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0\) (read from the PDF's text; the survey's printed form uses \(x_t\) in place of \(x_0\), flagged as a likely slip elsewhere in this page's series). SD 1.5 is trained to predict \(\epsilon\), not \(v\); the prediction-type switch above shows what changes in the code if a model were trained the other way.

Schedulers

Classes in diffusers/schedulers. Each turns \(\hat\epsilon\) into \(x_{t-1}\) its own way; the trained UNet is the same for all of them.

DDIM
Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (Song, Meng and Ermon 2021). This page. A non-Markovian reverse process that reaches the same trained objective as DDPM but allows a deterministic step (\(\eta=0\)) and far fewer sampling steps. Class: DDIMScheduler.
DDPM
Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (Ho et al. 2020). Computes \(\mu_\theta(x_t,t)\) directly (survey Eq. 6) and always samples with fresh noise; equivalent to this page's DDIM update at \(\eta=1\). Class: DDPMScheduler.
PNDM
Pseudo Numerical methods for Diffusion Models (Liu et al. 2022). SD 1.5's actual default scheduler, not DDIM; reuses earlier \(\hat\epsilon\) values to take better steps. Not read on this page; the X-Ray's route treats DDIM as the clean illustration of the general pattern PNDM refines. Class: PNDMScheduler.
